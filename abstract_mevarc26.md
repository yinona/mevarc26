# MeVArc 2026 — abstract

## Title

Does conditioning leave a structural memory? Large-area evidence for the dislocation picture

## Abstract

Dislocation dynamics are a leading candidate mechanism for high-field
conditioning. The mobile-dislocation-density fluctuation model predicts that
conditioning changes the near-surface dislocation structure [1]. Cross-sectional
microscopy of conditioned copper later revealed a dislocation-denuded zone
within the top ~100–200 nm, but these measurements were limited to small,
isolated regions [2]. A large-area structural comparison was still missing. We
addressed this gap using electron backscatter diffraction to map
millimeter-scale regions of the copper cathode from a sloped-anode pulsed-DC
experiment [3,4]. The sloped geometry produced a known gradient of
electric-field exposure across a single electrode. Intragrain misorientation, a
proxy for geometrically necessary dislocation content, was ~75% higher in
field-exposed regions than in unexposed references, and the high-misorientation
fraction increased approximately eightfold [3]. Grain size and the high-angle
grain-boundary network remained unchanged, indicating that field exposure
modified the grain interiors without substantially altering the underlying grain
structure [3]. The spatial ordering of the microstructural changes followed the
simulated degree of conditioning across the cathode [3,4], suggesting that the
evolving subsurface dislocation population may provide a microstructural basis
for conditioning. I will also discuss how depth-resolved microscopy of
conditioned samples could relate near-surface dislocation depletion to the
large-area structural changes observed here.
