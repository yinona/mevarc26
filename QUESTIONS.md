17. **Slide 14: dielectric picture vs Debye-like length (one paragraph, as requested).** The left panel and the analogy strip use a dielectric: bound dipoles polarize in the applied field and reduce the interior field to E₀/ε_r. In Livne–Schiller–Moshe (PRE 107, 055004) that is the dipole regime of mechanical screening, so the drawing and the manuscript's dipole-regime sentence (cond26 L379–381) agree. The length quoted on the same slide, ℓ_D ≈ 1/(4.2√ρ) ≈ 25 nm, comes from Groma's Debye-like theory (PRL 96, 165503), in which the screening is monopole-like: a depleted or enriched layer is screened by the surrounding ensemble over a finite length. The two pictures are not contradictory, but they are different regimes, and a listener who knows both papers (Moshe's group, or a Uppsala theorist) may ask which one the 25 nm belongs to. Nothing on the slide is wrong; the only risk is the word "Debye-like" next to a dielectric drawing. Options: [ ] leave as is and answer if asked ("the drawing is the polarization analogy; the 25 nm is Groma's Debye-like length for the internal stress of a depleted layer"); [ ] change the strip's "Debye length ↔ ℓ_D" to "screening length (Groma) ↔ ℓ_D ≈ 25 nm" so the slide does not name Debye. Recommended: leave as is, keep the answer ready (QA_PREP Q&A update, Q3).
